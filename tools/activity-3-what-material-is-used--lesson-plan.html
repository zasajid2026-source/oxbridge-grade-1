Lesson Plan

6.4 — Activity 3 — What material is used to make a pail, an eraser, a book?
Unit 6 — Materials · Book page 52

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the different kinds of materials.
  • List some common properties of materials.
  • Use materials to make different products.

Introduction

Ask: Everything is made of some material. Can we name the material of the things in the pictures?

Teaching Steps

  • Read the book's 'Do you know?': Mercury is the only metal that remains in liquid form at room temperature, at around 25 degrees.
  • Open the book at Activity 3. Look at the pictures: chair, window, book, tyre, gloves, coin, pencil, nail, eraser and pail.
  • Read the book's three questions: What material is used to make a pail? What material is used to make an eraser? What material is used to make a book?
  • Children answer the three questions for the pictured objects, using the materials learned in this unit.

Board Work

Activity 3 questions: pail? eraser? book? Do you know: mercury — only metal liquid at room temperature (around 25 degrees).

Class Activity

This lesson is the book's Activity 3. Children look at the pictures and answer the three questions about the materials used to make a pail, an eraser and a book.

Assessment

  • Which metal remains liquid at room temperature?
  • What are the three questions of Activity 3?

Homework

Write the mercury fact in your notebook.

Conclusion

Mercury is a special metal: it stays liquid at room temperature, at around 25 degrees.
